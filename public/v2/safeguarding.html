Safeguarding

Not a policy
in a drawer.

Safeguarding is what happens at 8:45 on a wet Tuesday when a child arrives with an inhaler and a grandparent nobody recognises turns up at pick-up. Wigglekit is built for that moment — and there are three things it simply will not let you skip.

Enforced, not suggested

Three things you can’t click past.

Most software warns you. These are refused by the server — there is no “continue anyway”.

🔒 Enforced

No consent, no dose

A dose cannot be recorded against a medication that has no parental consent on file. Not a warning — the record is rejected. Every dose that is given goes on the MAR with the name of the person who gave it.

🔒 Enforced

No trip without every consent

A trip cannot be marked ready or completed while a single child on it is missing consent. The ratio target flags before you leave, not after.

🔒 Enforced

No lapsed certificate on a rota

Switch on compliance and a staff member whose first aid or safeguarding training has expired cannot be rostered onto a session. Expiry dates chase themselves before they lapse.

On the day

Who’s here, who’s left, and with whom.

Taken on a phone at the door, not on a clipboard that gets left in the hall.

Registers, ratios & collection

Present, absent, collected

Every child is signed in and signed out, and the register is the record of exactly who was in your care and when.

A collection password

Set a password on a child and the person collecting gives it at the door. If they can’t, the child doesn’t leave — and staff have a script, not a judgement call.

Collected by — recorded

The name of the adult who collected is recorded against the child, and only people on that family’s list can be chosen. Walk-home permission is a separate, explicit flag.

Allergies on the row

Allergies, medication, SEND support and care notes sit on the child’s row on the register — visible to the staff running that session, not to everyone with a login.

Ratios, calculated live

Adult-to-child ratios update as children are signed in and flag the moment you drop below what you set — on site and on trips.

Roll call, any time

Run a headcount mid-session or at every stage of a trip, and it’s recorded — so “we counted” is evidenced rather than remembered.

When something isn’t right

One button, and the right person knows.

Staff don’t decide whether a concern is serious enough to pass on. They log it; your safeguarding lead decides.

Log a concern

Your lead is alerted, every time

Submitting a concern notifies your named safeguarding lead automatically. Nothing sits in a drawer waiting for someone to notice it.

Your categories, your protocol

The concern categories and the “what to do now” steps shown on the form are yours to write — your wording, your deadline, not generic advice.

The right numbers, ready

Your LADO, MASH and out-of-hours numbers for every council you work in, on the form and on the exported record, live for click-to-call. Nobody looks up a number calmly.

What you did about it

The decision and the action taken are recorded on the same record as the concern, so the two never come apart — and the whole thing exports as a PDF.

Accidents, in writing

Log an accident and the parent is emailed and notified in the app — and chased until they acknowledge it. A word at the door is not a record.

Timestamped and attributed

Every concern, accident, dose and register entry carries who recorded it and when. That’s as much for your staff’s protection as anyone’s.

The people you put in front of children

Cleared to start — or not started.

Safer recruitment records kept as one list per person, not four spreadsheets and a folder.

One record per person

DBS, references, right to work, qualifications and induction on a single record, with expiry dates that chase themselves before they lapse.

A gate, not a checklist

A record short of what you require reads Start on hold. Nobody works unsupervised until it reads Cleared to start.

Policies, read and confirmed

Publish your safeguarding policy with version history, assign it to roles, and see who has actually read and confirmed the current version.

No shared logins

Everyone who sees a child’s record has their own account, so every view and every edit has a name against it.

Only what their role needs

A None / View / Edit matrix per area. Medical detail reaches the staff running that session. Money doesn’t reach staff at all.

Training on the same record

Assign safeguarding, first aid and food hygiene from a built-in course library and keep completions and certificates against the person.

Images and information

Consent-first, and yours to control.

Photo consent, respected

A child whose photo consent is off is excluded from shared moments — and staff see the flag before the picture is taken, not after.

You are the data controller

Your families’ data is yours. It is not pooled with other providers, sold, or shared. Held in line with UK GDPR, encrypted in transit and at rest.

Records that outlast the term

Registers, concerns, accidents and medication logs are kept properly and exportable, so an inspection or a subject access request isn’t an archaeology project.

Where software stops

Safeguarding is yours. We just refuse to let it be sloppy.

Wigglekit records, prompts and, in the three places above, refuses. It does not decide whether someone is suitable to work with children, notice that a quiet child has gone quieter, or supervise a session. Those are judgements made by trained people, and no software should claim otherwise.

What it does do is remove the excuses. The consent was recorded or it wasn’t. The register says who collected her. The concern reached your lead the same afternoon, with the time on it. The certificate was in date, or the rota wouldn’t take them.

You still need a named safeguarding lead, trained staff, a policy your team has actually read, and the local numbers to hand. Set those up on day one — the system will hold you to them afterwards.
