# Naming agent 4 of 5 - SHORT, GLOBALLY PRONOUNCEABLE names

Written overnight 30 Sep to 1 Oct 2026. Angle: 4-7 letter names that work across languages, as an app icon and as a spoken / voice-searched name. This is NOT legal clearance. Nothing was registered, bought or contacted. No app code was touched.

## 1. Product understanding

ActivityOS (customer-facing site text already says "Activly", nav still says "Message ActivityOS") is a multi-tenant UK platform for children's activity and childcare providers: holiday camps, after-school clubs, schools, freelancers, companies and franchise networks. Six portals (company, franchise, freelancer operators; staff; parents "custdash"; platform/HQ). Operators get blocks and listings, bookings, registers, timetables, ratios and groups, trips and consent, meals, safeguarding incidents/medication/first aid, discount codes and referrals, invoices, reconciliation, payroll and payslips, timesheets and leave, compliance certificates, a Teaching Hub / Learning Hub (lessons, worksheets, quizzes, games), CRM/leads, messaging and an AI assistant, plus Xero/QuickBooks/Sage links and Stripe. Parents book, pay (incl. Tax-Free Childcare), manage child profiles and give trip consent. HQ has Sales, Leads, Support, Providers and an "International Expansion" view (research on Ireland, Belgium, Netherlands, France, Germany, Spain, New Zealand, Denmark, USA; the report currently argues fix the UK funnel first). The UI ships in 11 languages (en ar ur pl ro cy bn pa pt es fr). Tone: warm, trustworthy, UK-rooted but exportable; users are parents, children, provider owners and staff. So the name must be easy to say to a parent at a school gate, survive Arabic/Urdu/Bengali/Punjabi/Polish/Romanian/Welsh speakers reading it, and stretch from "booking" to "payroll" to "learning".

## Headline finding (read this first)

Every short name I tried is already registered as a .com, and almost every real-word or short coined name is also taken on .co.uk and .app (RDAP says registered; a control nonsense domain returned 404, so the method does distinguish). Only a handful of coined 5-6 letter names still show .co.uk, .uk, .app, .io and .co as unregistered. Those are the clearest names but they carry no built-in meaning. Real-word-ish names (Amiko, Sunu, Naya) have better meaning but crowded registers and taken domains. The UK trade mark register could not be read at all (see section 4), so NO candidate can be called GREEN. Best clearance rating achieved is AMBER.

### Recommendation

## 4. What I could not check

- UK IPO trade mark register (trademarks.ipo.gov.uk): HTTP 403 via WebFetch; unreadable. TMview (tmdn.org) returned an empty page (JavaScript app). WIPO Global Brand Database and EUIPO eSearch were not readable either. The only "trade mark" evidence is WebSearch snippets (IPO journal pages, trademarkdir.com, IP Australia), which found no exact-name marks for the 12 but are NOT a register search. Unverified: any UK/EU/US/international mark in classes 9, 35, 41, 42, 43 or 45 for any of the 30 names, including Amiko Digital Health, Plyra Ltd, Kivoro Ltd, Sunu Pay and the Naya family.
- Companies House: I read only the first results page per name via WebFetch's summary; I did not open company records, so sector (SIC) of same-name companies such as KIVORO LTD, PLYRA LTD and OYLA MAGAZINE LTD is not verified. No Scottish/Irish/EU company registers, no Companies House officers/PSC check.
- Domains: RDAP gives registered/unregistered only; a registered .com may be parked, for sale, or live; 404 is not proof a domain can be bought (reserved/premium). I did not check .com owners, aftermarket prices, social handles (Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn), App Store or Google Play names directly. Some RDAP calls were rate-limited (429) and are marked unknown (Kora .app, Sunu .uk).
- Web and app conflicts: WebSearch is coarse; it returns nothing for rare strings even when a product exists. I did not search Google Play/App Store directly, nor non-English web.
- Multilingual audit: from my own knowledge, not verified by native speakers or dictionaries; Hindi, Turkish, Mandarin, Japanese, Italian and German checks especially should be confirmed. Welsh, Polish, Romanian and the four South Asian languages need a native pass on the final shortlist (see docs/i18n-native-review.md for the project's process).
- Pronunciation and voice-search tests (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant) were not run.
- Not legal clearance. A solicitor or trade mark attorney must run a full clearance before any launch.
