## 1. What UK children love, by band

### 1.1 Cross-band facts
- 89% of UK 3-17s play games online [Verified, S1]. Roblox reaches 61% of UK 8-14s monthly [Verified, S1]. Minecraft, Fortnite and FIFA are named favourites in Ofcom's qualitative sample; Minecraft is played most frequently "because of its creative aspects" [Verified, S1, S2].
- Childwise Summer 2025 (n=1,360, ages 3-12): Roblox top for 7-12 mobile games; YouTubers named include MrBeast and Chuff/Chuffsters; hot topics include Labubu, KPop Demon Hunters, football [Verified, S3]. Ages 7-12 are also into K-/C-drama (teens especially) [Verified, S3].
- UK toys 2025 (Circana): market +6% year to Sept; Pokemon top-gaining property (strategic trading cards); Lego, Pokemon, Stitch, F1 cross generations; Squishmallows and Toniebox among top sellers; building sets 18% of purchases; Bluey number 2 in pre-school toys behind Paw Patrol [Verified, S4].
- Trend toward calm/cosy: collectible "small but meaningful" figures (Sonny Angel, Molang pastel sleepy figures, blind-box, seasonal releases) [Verified, S5]. Cosy games (Animal Crossing) appeal because "no timer, no violence, no sense of impending doom"; fans love the "collection of little buddies" [Verified, S6, S7].
- Collecting is a core hook: Pokemon was built on completion and companionship (Tajiri's insect collecting); designs prioritise a distinct silhouette and readable expression [Verified, S8].

Motivation dips after the move to secondary; autonomy support slows the decline [Verified, S10]. Secondary teacher sources say sticker charts and marble jars fall flat while XP economies, squads, milestone badges and earned privileges work [Verified as a blog claim, S11; weak evidence].

### 1.3 The "babyish cliff" and how bridge products cope
- I found no rigorous UK study quantifying it [Verified absence of hit, S12]. Advice that surfaced: subtle feedback (muted tick) beats "a circus of stickers and confetti" [Verified, S12; weak source].
- Bridge patterns [Unverified interpretation, supported by product facts]:
  - Duolingo: one mascot, one emotionally expressive voice ("expressive, playful, embracing, worldly"), designed to be encouraging, never threatening [Verified, S13]. Ages stay together because tone is wry and adult-readable, art is simple.
  - Wordle: no profile, no ads, once a day, spoiler-free share grid; playable by schoolchildren and adults [Verified, S14]. Lesson: a shared ritual and minimal chrome cross age.
  - Minecraft: same world; younger kids build, older kids survive/automate; mode = maturity dial [Verified, S15].
  - Among Us/Fall Guys: blob characters, simple controls, social play; universal because no prior skill needed [Verified, S16 summary]. Cosmetics carry identity.
- Design implication: keep ONE world and ONE mascot, but give it two "voices/skins" (Junior and Explorer/Field Notes) rather than two products. Age-gate tone, not content. Let the child pick the skin (autonomy).

### 1.4 SEND-friendly aesthetics
- Soft palettes, clear visual feedback, predictable patterns, adjustable difficulty, reduced sensory load; calming predictable sounds or user-selected sound themes; nature settings may be calming [Verified, S17; small studies, moderate evidence].
- Rules to adopt [Unverified]: no flashing (aurora must be slow and can be turned off), a "quiet mode" (no music, reduced motion), consistent layout of the place map, no surprise loud sounds, dyslexia-friendly type, colour never the only signal.

## 2. World-building for a learning platform

- Intrinsic integration beats chocolate-covered broccoli: if game mechanics ARE the learning content (Habgood and Ainsworth's Zombie Division, ages 7-11), motivation and learning improve; a "sugar coating" quiz does not [Verified, S18]. Penguin Slide (sliding on the right number) is already intrinsically integrated. Rule: the world should give the maths a reason (the numbers are the ice/tide/stars mechanic), not wrap it.
- Streaks work by loss aversion; freezes cut churn (~21% quoted); but streaks can encourage speed-running without learning [Verified as blog-level claims, S19]. Use forgiving streaks ("rest days"), never guilt. Mascot never shames [S13].
- Unified world vs per-subject worlds vs seasons [Unverified recommendation, reasoning from Pokemon/Animal Crossing]:
  - Per-subject worlds multiply art cost and fragment the collection. One world with subject "places" gives one identity, one mascot, one home.
  - Seasons (time-limited events) add freshness cheaply: re-skin the same map; suits Animal Crossing-style calendar [S6]. Avoid FOMO expiry for children; make seasonal items re-earnable.
- Collectibles, pets, cosmetics: Pokemon-style completion and companionship [S8]; blind-box seasonal collecting is a live trend [S5]. But: no random paid loot (UK regulatory and ethical risk) [Unverified]; earn by learning effort, not luck or money. Show "field guide" pages with facts (a natural learning payload).
- Base-building: Minecraft creativity and Animal Crossing decoration are the top calm/creative loops [S1, S6, S15]. Give every child a small "home" they decorate with earned items.
- Narrative progression: light, episodic, optional (Bluey succeeds via short self-contained episodes [S9]). Story beats unlock at mastery milestones, not walls.
- Anti-broccoli checklist: (1) the maths is the mechanic; (2) rewards are cosmetic, not content-gating; (3) the world is worth visiting without a quiz; (4) older skin drops cuteness not depth; (5) no dark patterns.

## 3. IP and legal (not legal advice; no UKIPO search was run)

- Club Penguin: Disney shut the original in March 2017 and later the fan clones; Club Penguin Rewritten was shut in April 2022, site seized by City of London Police, three arrests, ~11m registered users [Verified, S20]. Lesson: do not echo Club Penguin's look, igloo/puffle/coin-and-room-decorating combination, or name patterns.
- Pingu: the PINGU mark is registered (US reg 7202239, owner Joker Inc / "Pygos Group", Mattel-linked); revival in development with Aardman [Verified, S21]. Avoid Pingu-like names ("Ping-", "Pin-"), clay/stop-motion look, gibberish-speech gimmick.
- "Penguin": Penguin Books' penguin-in-lozenge logo is registered and cannot be used commercially without permission; PRH has consolidated to one core penguin mark [Verified, S22]. The mascot's shape is generic (a penguin), but ORANGE oval/lozenge roundel, orange-and-white palette and a book/publisher context are Penguin Books territory [Unverified inference]. Do not use "Penguin" in a company/brand word mark for books/education services without clearance; our brand is "Penguin Slide" (a game title) so a clearance check for classes 9, 41 is required [Unverified, needs a solicitor].
- Happy Feet (Warner Bros film) and "Percy": I did not find sources. From general knowledge [Unverified]: Happy Feet is a Warner/Village Roadshow film franchise (tap-dancing emperor penguins); "Percy" is famous as Percy the Small Engine (Thomas) and Percy Pig (M&S). Avoid the name Percy, dance-centred penguin, and realistic CGI emperor look.
- UKIPO basics: registration costs "at least £205" and IPO checks conflicts with same/similar existing marks; ~3-4 months if unopposed [Verified, S23]. Invented, inherently distinctive words are easiest to register; similarity judged on visual, phonetic and conceptual overall impression; closeness of goods matters [Verified, S24]. Descriptive names (Times Table Penguin) are weak.
- Ownability recipe [Unverified]:
  1. Name the mascot with an invented or oblique word, not "X Penguin". Clearance-check phonetics.
  2. Give a signature silhouette: navy body, graduation cap with gold tassel (already ours), a distinctive extra feature (e.g. a small brass pocket-compass or lantern on the flipper).
  3. Supporting cast are NOT penguin-only and NOT puffle-like round blobs; mix species with strong silhouettes (Pokemon rule [S8]).
  4. Avoid Disney-style princess/rounded-eye house style; commission a distinctive illustrator and keep signed assignment of copyright.
  5. File UK trade mark for mascot name + logo (classes 9, 41, 16 if printables) after a professional search; register design of the character as artistic copyright evidence (dated concept sheets).
  6. Do not mimic Bluey/Pokemon/Animal Crossing mechanics wholesale in art; mechanics are free, expression is protected [Unverified].
  7. Fan-engagement policy: allow non-commercial fan art to build community but state rules (Disney's crackdown timing followed monetisation with ads [S20]).

## 4. Culture and inclusion

- Representation gap: only 5% of UK children's books published in 2019 had a Black, Asian or minority ethnic main character while 33.5% of pupils are from minority ethnic backgrounds; seeing yourself reflected improves engagement [Verified, S25].
- Practical rules [Unverified, best practice]:
  - Cast: mix skin tones, hair, disability (wheelchair user, hearing aid, cane), body sizes, family shapes; none singled out by stereotype. Cast members have hobbies, not "cultural jobs".
  - Names: pronounceable, drawn from many origins (Ayo, Priya, Tomasz, Mei, Kit, Zainab, Callum, Noor); avoid all-Anglo or tokenistic.
  - Food: include jollof, dal, dumplings, fish fingers, falafel, fruit; halal/vegetarian by default in the world; avoid pork and alcohol imagery.
  - Festivals: religious neutrality by default: "Lantern Night", "Harvest Day", "Midwinter Glow", "Spring Bloom". Let families toggle real-calendar events (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Hanukkah, Vaisakhi, Lunar New Year) as opt-in cosmetic events rather than baking one in.
  - Avoid stereotype hazards: Arab/Asian "exotic" set dressing, "tribal" patterns, Native headdress cosmetics, Santa/Christmas as default winter.
  - Right-to-left and other languages: draw props with no embedded English text (or use swappable text layers) so Arabic/Urdu/Hebrew/Welsh can be added; test mirrored layouts; symbols over words where possible; avoid puns that cannot translate. Arrows and progress bars must mirror in RTL.
  - Penguin gender: mascot un-gendered; cast balanced; names and colours not gender-coded (the Ofcom/Childwise chart-toppers cross gender [S1, S3]).

## 5. Three theme directions

Brand palette base (from brief): navy #14206b / royal #2a3fa0, violet #8b6cf0, gold #f5c542, snow #f4f1ff. Exact tokens should come from the app CSS variables; hex values here are illustrative.

Moodboard sketches: `docs/games-research/deep/moodboards/three-directions.html` (SVG).

### A. Aurora Station: "The Polar Research Base"
- Pitch: You join the penguin scientists at a glowing Arctic/Antarctic research station where every subject is a lab, and the aurora brightens as you learn.
- World map: Hub "Aurora Deck" (home base, bedroom-style room you decorate); Maths Ice Shelf (times tables, sliding rink); Science Lab Dome; Reading Reef Library Cave; History Ice Core Tunnel (dig layers); Geography Weather Mast; Languages Radio Room; Arts Ice Studio; Exam Summit (Y10-13 revision).
- Cast (all original, not all penguins):
  1. Captain-cap mascot (name TBC, e.g. "Tassel"): curious, encouraging, gently wry.
  2. Dr Nkechi Seal: elderly seal glaciologist, dry humour, loves facts.
  3. Kit and Juno: twin arctic-fox engineers, tinker-y, rivalry-banter.
  4. Bea the beluga: radio operator, voice of announcements, sings numbers.
  5. Mo the walrus: cook/quartermaster, snacks from many cuisines.
  6. Ash the snowy owl: navigator, quiet, patient (SEND-safe "calm guide").
  7. Rook: a tiny skua who "borrows" things; mischief without menace.
- Palette: navy #14206b sky, violet #8b6cf0 aurora, mint-teal accent #6ee7c8 (aurora only), gold #f5c542 station lights, snow #e8eeff.
- Sound world: soft glockenspiel, wind chimes, distant whale calls, ice "clinks"; low drone for focus mode.
- Times tables fit: Penguin Slide on the ice shelf: pick the lane matching the multiple; stars/aurora energy for streaks. Native fit; expected.
- Progression: aurora brightness meter and station rooms unlock; collect "expedition badges" and Field Guide of Arctic species; decorate dorm pod; penguin outfits (scarf, goggles, parka).
- Tone by band: Y1-2 storybook animals; Y3-6 explorer log; Y7-9 "Field Notes" skin with data readouts; Y10-13 dark "Observatory" skin with minimal mascot.
- Sample copy: "Ice is thin at 7 times 8. Slide, then check your lantern: 56!" / Y8: "Streak: 6 days. Base is warm. Tomorrow's mission: fractions." / Y12: "Revision block done. 42 minutes logged."
- Risks: near-Club Penguin territory (snow, penguins, igloos) and Pingu/Happy Feet; polar is what everyone expects; Arctic/Antarctic mixing (penguins and polar bears do not co-exist; teacher pedantry) [Unverified]; "cold" visual tone; climate-anxiety themes for older kids.

### B. Skyharbour: "The Sky Archipelago"
- Pitch: Floating islands linked by kites and lantern-bridges; each subject is an island and the penguin is the ferry-keeper who learns to fly a glider.
- World map: Hub "Skyharbour" dock; Number Isle (maths, Penguin Slide as wind-slide ramps); Story Isle (English, cloud library); Wonder Isle (science, storm lab); Time Isle (history, sunken clocks); Globe Isle (geography, wind map); Song Isle (languages/music); Summit Spire (exams).
- Cast: 1) Mascot penguin ferry-keeper (out-of-habitat joke: "penguins cannot fly, so I built a glider"); 2) Zephyra, wind-spirit kite-maker (they/them); 3) Old Gantry, tortoise harbourmaster; 4) Pip and Pop, cloud-mice postal couriers; 5) Amara, girl cartographer with a whale-shaped airship; 6) Sol, boy lamplighter who runs cloud-lanterns; 7) Marlow, gruff albatross weather forecaster.
- Palette: royal #5b7cff sky, violet dusk #8b6cf0, warm peach horizon #f7c9a8, gold lanterns #f5c542, leaf green #5fcf8b.
- Sound world: harp, whistle, gentle woodwind, wind swell; airy reverb.
- Times tables fit: Slide becomes "wind-slide" on cloud-rails; landing on the correct island pad; bridges lit by multiples. Penguin sliding stays.
- Progression: reclaim floating islands (each island fully mastered = a garden), collect kites, glider skins and cloud pets; seasons via weather.
- Tone by band: Y1-2 picture-book gentle; Y3-6 adventurous; Y7-9 Ghibli-adjacent sketch style; Y10-13 pared-back "atlas" skin.
- Sample copy: "The wind is up! Ride the 6 times table to Story Isle."
- Risks: brand penguin looks out of place (needs a story reason); heights/fall imagery can worry anxious or vertigo-prone children (SEND) [Unverified]; Ghibli/Zelda/Sky-type resemblance; higher art cost for many islands; Peter Pan/Cloud-city cliches.

### C. Lantern Cove: "The Cosy Harbour Town You Tend"
Chosen because the evidence (calm cosy games, collecting, decorating, creative sandbox, Roblox-style identity, small-format collectible trend) points to a place-you-own rather than a place-you-visit [S1, S5, S6, S8, S15].
- Pitch: A small lantern-lit fishing town on a northern-ish coast where the penguin is the harbour keeper; learning lights the lanterns and restores the town, and every child owns a cottage to decorate.
- World map (subjects as places): Lighthouse Observatory (hub, streak lantern); The Counting House and Rink (maths, Penguin Slide on the harbour ice-slide/ramps); The Bookshop Boat (English); The Tidepool Lab (science); The Museum of Finds (history: dig beach layers); Harbour Chart Room (geography); The Bandstand (languages/music); The Workshop (design/computing); Cliff-top Study Hall (Y10-13 revision, quiet).
- Cast (5-8):
  1. Mascot penguin harbour keeper (name TBC): patient, curious, gets things a bit wrong on purpose so you can help.
  2. Auntie Nia, puffin ferry captain: warm, chatty, loves numbers.
  3. Baz, otter fishmonger: jokey; his stall sells "number fish".
  4. Ms Okonkwo-Reyes, heron librarian: dry, precise; the calm voice for older bands.
  5. Twins Sami and Lin, crab kids: play-fight over answers; gentle rivalry.
  6. Old Whit, seal lighthouse-keeper: night-time story teller; quiet mode.
  7. Fern, hedgehog carpenter who builds decor you unlock.
  8. The Glimmers: tiny original lantern-creatures you collect (field guide; Pokemon-like collecting, but original, non-battle).
- Palette: dusk navy #2b2f8f, violet sky #8b6cf0, lantern gold #f5c542, sea blue #1b5f9e, snow #f4f1ff; brick-red roofs used sparingly.
- Sound world: acoustic guitar/ukulele-lite, marimba, harbour bells, gulls, soft waves; a "night" quiet mix.
- Times tables fit: Penguin Slide as the harbour ramp: slide onto the right lantern; each table lit = a Glimmer to collect; the "market" uses multiples (6 crates of 7). Native to the town's economy.
- Progression: lighten lanterns (mastery map), collect Glimmers (field guide with real facts), decorate cottage and mascot outfits, seasonal town events (opt-in real festivals, plus neutral ones), light story chapters per lantern. No pay-to-win, no random paid loot, streak = "lamp stays lit", rest days allowed.
- Tone by band: Y1-2 storybook cottage; Y3-6 collect-and-decorate; Y7-9 "Harbour Log" skin (muted, journal look); Y10-13 "Study Hall" skin with the lantern as a quiet timer and cosmetic profile only.
- Sample copy: "Ferry leaves in one tick. 9 crates times 4 fish? Slide to 36!" / Y9: "Lamp lit: day 12. Nia says you have earned a new roof tile." / Y13: "Study Hall is open. Ready for a 25-minute block?"
- Risks: cosy = slower pace (less thrill for some 9-11 boys per stereotype [Unverified, avoid]); resemblance to Animal Crossing (villager-town + decorating) needs a distinct look and mechanics; town maintenance can feel like chores; Glimmers vs Pokemon similarity; more systems to build (home, decor).

## 7. Recommendation

Build **C, Lantern Cove**, with the penguin as harbour keeper, but keep polar cues from A (snowy roofs, aurora sky as a night-mode backdrop, slide-on-ice for the game) so the current Penguin Slide and brand penguin still feel native. Borrow B's weather/seasonal idea as events. Confidence: 65% that C beats A with real children (medium). Grounds: cosy, collect, decorate loops are the best-supported cross-age hooks; it is the most ownable; it lets tone shift via skins. Weakest link: no direct UK evidence for this exact world; hence run the tests below before commissioning full art. Confidence that A alone is too generic and legally closest to Club Penguin: 75%.

## 8. Five low-cost tests

1. Card-sort concept survey (parents + children 5-16, 150 respondents via existing tutoring families): three moodboards (this file's SVG), rank + "which would you like to open again tomorrow"; segment by band and gender. Cost: a Google Form + 2 days.
2. Paper prototype of the town map with 12 children per band (Y1, Y4, Y8, Y11): children place subjects and choose cottage decor; observe delight and "babyish" comments. Half a day each.
3. A/B skin test in-app: same Penguin Slide with (a) polar skin and (b) harbour skin for 2 weeks, 50/50; measure session length, day-7 return, and correct-answer rate (learning, not just time). Guard: watch for streak-driven speed-running [S19].
4. "Babyish" thermometer: show older bands (Y7-13) Junior vs Explorer skin of the same screen, ask 3 Likert items (looks for me / for younger kids / I would use it in front of friends). Cheap; decides the skin split.
5. Legal/naming pre-screen: 5 shortlisted mascot names + cast names through a UKIPO search and a solicitor's 1-hour clearance, plus a small parent poll on name pronounceability and cultural neutrality (also SEND parents panel for the quiet/reduced-motion features).

## 9. Ten decisions for the owner

1. Pick the direction (recommend C; or approve a one-week A-vs-C test).
2. Approve renaming the mascot to an invented, non-"Penguin" name and dropping "Percy"-style names; approve legal clearance spend.
3. Should the game title stay "Penguin Slide"? (Trade-mark clearance for classes 9/41; consider "Slide" plus mascot name.)
4. One world with two skins (Junior / Explorer) or only one look? (Recommend two skins, child-chosen.)
5. Collectibles: earned only, or also shop? (Recommend earned only; no paid random loot.)
6. Streak policy: forgiving with rest days and freezes, no guilt copy.
7. Festivals: neutral defaults with opt-in real-calendar events; who curates and reviews?
8. Art budget/model: single commissioned illustrator with assignment of copyright vs asset pack; cost ceiling for a first vertical slice (hub + maths).
9. SEND commitments: quiet mode, reduced motion, dyslexia fonts, no flashing as launch requirements.
10. Localisation scope: RTL and Welsh in v1 (text-free art) or later?
