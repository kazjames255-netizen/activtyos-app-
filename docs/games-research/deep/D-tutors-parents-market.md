### Persona A: "Priya", solo tutor (freelancer, 12-20 pupils, Y3 to GCSE)
- Context: 40-60 min sessions; games are a 3-8 min warm-up, a 5-min plenary, or a homework top-up. Pays commission or software fees; margin matters.

  2. When I finish, give me evidence I can paste to the parent proving what changed.
  3. When a child is stuck, let me control difficulty and stop the game without a fight.
  4. When the parent asks "is it worth it", show me a chart I can screenshot.
  5. Keep the child from seeing other children's names or being able to chat.

### Persona B: "Marcus", small tuition company / franchise owner (5-40 tutors)
- JTBD: standardise quality across tutors; see which pupils and tutors are active; brand the parent report; use consistent curriculum tagging; prove retention. Wants per-tutor seats and an admin view. The tenancy model in this repo fits.

- JTBD:
  1. Reassure me the money is working: what was covered, what improved, what next.
  2. When my child plays at home, keep it ad-free, no purchases, capped time, and no nagging.
  3. Tell me in plain English, not a leaderboard rank.
  4. Do not make my anxious child feel timed or shamed (Mumsnet: "the timer just adds too much pressure").

### Persona E: School buyer (SLT / data protection lead / maths lead)
- JTBD: adopt only tools that pass safeguarding, DPIA and Cyber Essentials checks; integrate with the MIS rostering (Wonde/Clever/Google) so nobody creates accounts by hand; produce evidence for governors and Ofsted-style "impact"; keep price low and predictable (unlimited pupils flat fee is the norm: TTRS GBP 116.80). ## 3. Tutor-friendly game checklist and 30-second setup flow

### Checklist (score each game 0/1; ship only at 10+/12)
1. Setup under 30 s from "start session" to first question.
2. Pre-tagged to curriculum (KS/year/strand/NC objective) and to the tutor's current scheme of work.
3. Tutor controls: pause, skip, hide timer, change difficulty live, end for everyone.
4. Timer OFF by default for under-11s or struggling profile; opt-in.
5. Works on any device/browser, low bandwidth, survives disconnects (state resumes, no scoring penalty from lag).
6. No login for the child at the table: join by tutor's screen, QR, or 4-digit code; child logs in only for homework.
7. Safe names: tutor-issued nicknames or picked from a curated list; no free text.
8. Live insight while playing (who is stuck, misconception tags) visible only to the tutor.
9. Exportable evidence: PDF/CSV/one-tap parent message.
10. Printable companion (worksheet from the same questions).
11. Anti-cheat: server-side answer checking, no answer in client, per-child question shuffle, rate limits.
12. Zero ads, zero purchases, zero randomised paid rewards, screen-time caps configurable by tutor/parent.

### 30-second setup flow (session mode)
- 0-5 s: Tutor opens child's profile, taps "Warm-up". App pre-selects the last taught topic (from lesson notes / homework set) and a 5-minute game.
- 5-12 s: One toggle row shows suggested settings: mode (solo / tutor-vs-child / pair), difficulty (auto from history), timer (off/gentle/standard), length. Defaults are pre-filled; tutor only changes what they want.
- 12-20 s: Tap "Start". Child screen opens instantly via already-paired device, or shows a 4-digit code plus QR for a new device.
- 20-30 s: Game runs. Tutor sees a small side strip: live accuracy, misconception chips, "make easier / make harder / pause".
- After: one-tap "Send summary to parent" with pre-written 2-sentence update (see report 4a).

## 6. Pricing and packaging options with economics

Anchors: TTRS tutor GBP 3/pupil/yr; TTRS family GBP 9/yr; Blooket/Gimkit about USD 60/yr per teacher; Third Space GBP 3.5-6k/school/yr; Tassomai GBP 5-15/student/yr; MyTutor commission about 40% (reported). All [Verified] above unless flagged.

Assumptions for rough economics [Unverified, modelling only]: a solo tutor has 15 pupils; a company has 8 tutors and 100 pupils; churn 3%/month; payment fees 1.5% + 20p; cost to serve a pupil about GBP 0.30/month at the scale of this stack (compute, storage, support); if the platform already charges tutors a subscription, games can be bundled.

| Model | Price (illustrative) | Solo tutor (15 pupils) | Company (100 pupils) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Included in tutor subscription | Games in base plan, e.g. GBP 19-29/tutor/mo | GBP 228-348/yr | GBP 1.8-2.8k/yr (8 seats) | Lowest friction; differentiates the whole hub; predictable | Games cost is hidden; cannot upsell easily |
| B. Per-child add-on | GBP 1.50-3/child/mo (about 18-36/yr) | GBP 270-540/yr | GBP 1.8-3.6k/yr | Scales with value; TTRS is 3/yr so 18-36 must be justified by evidence reports | Tutors resist passing cost on; per-child feels metered |
| C. Flat "Games Pro" add-on per tutor | GBP 9-12/tutor/mo | GBP 108-144/yr | GBP 860-1,150/yr | Simple; matches Blooket/Gimkit annual price feel | Ignores pupil count |
| D. Parent-paid home pass | GBP 3-5/child/mo, tutor gets rev-share (30%) | Parent pays; tutor earns about GBP 9-18/child/yr | Same | Turns games into tutor income; solves "who pays" | Compliance load (parent billing, refunds); risk of looking like Prodigy if not careful |
| E. Freemium: free basic games + paid evidence/reporting | Free games, paid reports | Small conversion; ad-free | | Viral for tutors; sets trust | Needs volume; free-tier gutting is a hated pattern (Gimkit/Wordwall) |
| F. School site licence | GBP 1-3k/school/yr flat | n/a | n/a | TTRS-style flat; predictable | Long sales cycle; compliance heavy |

Cost-to-serve check: at GBP 0.30/pupil/month, B at GBP 2/child/mo yields about 85% gross margin; A at GBP 24/tutor/mo with 15 pupils costs about GBP 4.5/mo to serve, about 80% margin. [Unverified assumptions]

**Recommendation (confidence: medium, about 60%)**: Lead with **A+B hybrid**: games included for every tutor on a paid plan with a generous fair-use pupil cap (say 25 active pupils), then a **flat per-child overage** (about GBP 1/child/mo above the cap) and a **free "family view" for parents** (reports only, no purchases). Reasons: tutors judge value by parent retention, not by pupil count; per-child metering was not accepted where the incumbent is GBP 3/yr; "free child access, paid evidence" avoids the Prodigy trust trap. Delay Model D (parent billing) until trust is earned. Confidence low (about 35%) on the specific price points; validate in interviews (Q8, Q9).

Do NOT: show upgrade nags to children; free-tier gutting; auto-renew without a pre-renewal email.

## 8. What to build first for tutor pull, and 10 interview questions

### Build first (in order)
1. **"Warm-up in 30 s" session launcher**: one-tap topic-aware game from the child profile, tutor live strip with pause/easier/harder, timer off default. Reason: it is the moment-of-truth for tutors; competitors need setup.
2. **Two-sentence parent message + weekly report** auto-generated from game results and lesson topic. Reason: retention is the tutor's real KPI.
3. **2-3 launch games only**, chosen for evidence quality: retrieval quick-fire (tagged bank), a pair/versus mode (tutor vs child), and one story/puzzle for under-8s. Curriculum tags on every item (the repo already has a 4,679-question curriculum per memory notes).
4. **Guardrail panel** for parents (caps, no ads, no purchases).
Defer: leaderboards across children, avatar shops, parent billing, school MIS.

### Interview script (15-20 min, with 8-10 tutors and 5-6 parents)
1. Walk me through the last session where you used a game or digital activity. What did you do in the first two minutes?
2. How many minutes of a paid hour is a game "allowed" to take? Where does it go: start, middle, end, homework?
3. What is the last game/tool you stopped using, and exactly why?
4. What proof do you give parents today after a session? How long does it take, and where does it live (WhatsApp, email, PDF)?
5. Show me the most useful thing a parent ever said about progress. What convinced them?
6. What would make you refuse a game in a session (ads, timers, names, chat, sound, prizes)?
7. What would you need to see in a report to trust the score as evidence of teaching, not just gaming?
8. Would you pay per child, per tutor, or bundle inside your fees? At what monthly figure would you stop and think?
9. Would you let parents access play at home? What would worry you about it?
10. What device and connection do children actually use in your sessions, and what breaks?

Parent version: swap 1-5 for "What did your child last play; how did you feel; what would you want to see weekly; what would make you cancel; what would you pay?"

## Top 10 insights

1. Tutors are a real, priced buyer segment: TTRS sells a GBP 3 per pupil tutor plan with a 3-tutee minimum. Our value must clearly exceed that anchor by giving evidence, not content. [Verified: TTRS purchase page]
